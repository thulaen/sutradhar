# ADR-0005: Data and storage

Superseded in part by [ADR-0021](0021-version-1-product-stack-and-scope.md)
(background processing is PostgreSQL outbox and our own Axum/Tokio
scheduler, not Kafka or Airflow).

Decided by the owner on 8 November 2026. The job-storage choice is revised
by [ADR-0020](0020-version-1-documentation-and-architecture-baseline.md).

## Decision

- PostgreSQL is authoritative business storage, with row-level security
  forced on every product table and an app role that cannot bypass it.
- Numbered plain SQL migrations are applied by sqlx, with checksums, and
  checked by squawk.
- Kafka and Airflow own durable background delivery and orchestration.
  PostgreSQL may retain business checkpoints, idempotency records,
  reconciliation ledgers and explicitly approved component metadata, but
  not a competing durable-job queue or product outbox after cutover.
- The XenForo add-on's site change feed remains spec 0021's integration
  contract, not a product background-job executor.

The exact rules are in
[AGENTS.md section 5.2](../../AGENTS.md#52-data) and spec 0019.

On 21 November 2026 the owner approved parameterised transaction-local
site binding under DR-148. The site context section of [spec 0108]
describes the call and its failure cases. A bound value keeps the site
separate from the statement text and the context ends with the
transaction.

The same day the owner approved DR-150. First setup needs verified
checkpoints before a site exists. Approved app updates need their
own installation-level checkpoints and migration history after site
enrolment. [0030][install] and [spec 0108] define the roles and exact
records. Site policies still require an enrolled site.

## Why

Row-level security makes the database itself refuse cross-site access.
PostgreSQL provides strong transactions, crash recovery and backups for
business state. Kafka/Airflow provide one platform execution route, while
transactional idempotency and reconciliation records keep external side
effects accountable.

## Trade-offs

A business transaction and a broker publication are not one atomic
transaction by assertion. The design needs idempotent consumers,
replayable inputs and explicit reconciliation. PostgreSQL, broker and
orchestrator memory all count within resource admission.

## How we check it

- Checks refuse product tables without forced RLS and app roles that own
  tables or bypass RLS.
- Tests prove tenant isolation and transactional business writes.
- Migration tests cover compatibility, drain, replay, reconciliation,
  cutover and rollback with queued, running, dead-letter and duplicated
  work.
- Backup and restore tests include business state, idempotency and
  reconciliation evidence on every release.
